# Storytelling: Structure & Symbolism

Turn a draft or an idea into a story that is built on purpose and means something. Two things carry every piece: **structure** (the order and causality that pull a reader through) and **symbolism** (the concrete images that hold the meaning). This skill works on both, for any format — but it never guesses the format. It asks first.

## When to use

- The user brings a text and says it needs better build-up, flow, structure, or impact.
- The user wants to write something from an idea, brief, or rough notes.
- Any format: personal essay, blog/content, LinkedIn post, TikTok/Reels voiceover script, keynote or talk, customer/founder story, or fiction.

## Step 0 — Always ask about the brief first

Do not start rewriting until you know what you are building. Ask the user (group these into one quick exchange — use AskUserQuestion if helpful):

1. **Medium & length** — LinkedIn post? Voiceover script for a short video? Essay? Talk? Roughly how long?
2. **Audience** — Who reads/hears this, and what do they already believe?
3. **Purpose** — What should the audience *feel*, *think*, or *do* by the end? (A story without a target is decoration.)
4. **Voice** — Personal and honest? Conversational? Reflective? Punchy? The user's default leans personal, plain-spoken, and honest — not corporate, not hype. Confirm, don't assume.
5. **True or invented** — Is this a real experience or a constructed narrative? (Changes how far you can push events.)

If the user already answered some of these, don't re-ask — fill the gaps only.

## The three lenses (the core method)

Every piece gets read through three lenses, in this order. Structure first (so it holds together), then journey (so it moves), then symbolism (so it lands).

### Lens 1 — Structure (from Aristotle's *Poetics*)

The arrangement of events is the soul of the piece. A story is not a list of things that happened; it is a chain of cause and effect with a shape.

- **Unity of action.** One spine. Cut anything that doesn't serve the single thing the piece is about. Unity comes from *one action*, not from one narrator listing many events.
- **Beginning, middle, end — of adequate magnitude.** The piece must feel whole: a setup that needs resolving, a turn, a resolution. Not too thin to matter, not so sprawling the reader loses the thread.
- **The causal spine: "but / therefore", never "and then".** This is the single most useful structural test. If your beats connect with "and then… and then…", the piece is a list and it's flat. Rewrite so beats connect with **but** (conflict, a complication) or **therefore** (consequence). "I wanted X, *but* Y happened, *therefore* I did Z."
- **Complication and dénouement.** Know where the knot ties (the tension builds) and where it unties (the release). Aristotle says give special care to the untying — most weak drafts fumble the ending, not the start.
- **Probability and necessity.** Each beat should feel like it *had* to follow from the last. Surprise is good; arbitrariness is not. The best effect joins **the inevitable and the unexpected** — the turn you didn't see coming but believe instantly in hindsight.
- **Catharsis.** The piece should *do something* emotionally — release, recognition, a click of understanding. For non-fiction this is the moment the reader feels the point rather than being told it.

> Deep reference: [the_poetics_of_aristotle_reference.md](documents/the_poetics_of_aristotle_reference.md)

### Lens 2 — Journey & transformation (from Campbell's *Hero with a Thousand Faces*)

Even a 150-word LinkedIn post is a miniature journey. Someone leaves an ordinary state, meets a difficulty, and comes back changed with something to give. The monomyth is **separation → initiation → return**.

Map the piece onto the arc — loosely for short non-fiction, fully for narrative:
- **The call / the ordinary world** — where things start, and the disruption that begins the story. (In content: the hook + the situation.)
- **Crossing the threshold** — the decision or event that commits the protagonist (often *you*) to the road.
- **The road of trials** — the difficulty, the attempt, the failure. This is where most drafts are too thin. The struggle is the story; don't skip to the lesson.
- **The boon / transformation** — what was won, learned, or understood. The change must be *earned* by the trials, not announced.
- **The return** — bringing it back to the reader: the "so what" for *them*. The boon is only worth telling if it serves the audience.

For non-fiction, the key question is always: **what changed, and was the change earned on the page?** A personal essay or honest LinkedIn post is exactly this — a small death and rebirth, made useful to others.

> Deep reference: [the_hero_with_a_thousand_faces_reference.md](documents/the_hero_with_a_thousand_faces_reference.md)

### Lens 3 — Symbolism (from Jung's *Man and His Symbols*)

This is the layer most writing skips, and it's where meaning lives. A **symbol** is a concrete thing that carries more than its literal self — it points at something that can't be fully stated. Distinguish it from a **sign** (which just labels a known thing).

- **Find or plant the central image.** Abstract claims ("I was burned out", "trust matters") slide off the reader. A concrete image lodges. Burnout becomes *the unread Slack messages I stopped feeling guilty about*. Anchor the piece's meaning in one physical, specific, sensory object or moment.
- **Let the image recur.** A symbol earns weight by returning. Introduce it early, bring it back at the turn, pay it off at the end. The same object can mean something different by the close — that shift *is* the transformation made visible.
- **Use archetypes consciously, not as costume.** The threshold, the mentor, the shadow (the part of yourself you'd rather not show), the return home — these patterns resonate because they're shared. Reach for them when the piece needs depth, but ground them in real specifics so they don't read as cliché.
- **Show meaning through the concrete; don't explain it.** Trust the image to carry the idea. If you've built the symbol well, you can delete the sentence that explains what it means.

> Deep reference: [man_and_his_symbols_reference.md](documents/man_and_his_symbols_reference.md)

## The craft layer (sentence and moment level)

Once structure, journey, and symbol are set, sharpen the delivery:

- **Open with a real hook.** The first line should be concrete and signal what the piece is about. Kill vague teases ("Wait till you see this…", "Let me tell you a story about…"). Drop the reader into a specific moment or a sharp claim.
- **Write the last line first.** Decide where you're landing, then build backward so every beat points there. For looping short-form (Reels/TikTok), the last line should set up the first.
- **Find the lens (your angle).** The topic isn't enough — everyone has the topic. What's the specific, slightly unexpected angle only you would take? Pick the lens before drafting.
- **Zoom into the moment** instead of summarizing it. Five tools that make a scene live: **location** (where are we — one detail, not five), **action** (what you're physically doing), **thought** (the raw, unpolished thing you actually thought), **emotion** (show it in the body, don't name it), **dialogue** (what was actually said, kept tight and real). "I was nervous" → "I read the message three times and put the phone face-down."
- **Vary the rhythm.** Short sentences hit. Then a longer one that takes its time and lets the reader breathe before the next turn. Uniform sentence length reads as monotone and people scroll away.
- **Conversational tone by default** (unless the brief says otherwise). Write as if leaving a voice memo to one close friend. This is especially true for the user's usual work: personal, honest, plain — not corporate, not blue-collar cosplay, not hype.

## Workflow A — Revising a draft the user brings

1. **Run Step 0** if format/audience/purpose/voice aren't already clear.
2. **Diagnose against the three lenses.** Name the real problems in plain language, e.g.:
   - Structure: "This is 'and then' all the way down — there's no 'but'. Nothing's at stake."
   - Journey: "The transformation is announced in the last line but never earned — the struggle is one sentence."
   - Symbolism: "It's all abstract. There's no image to hold onto."
3. **Propose a restructure** before rewriting prose — show the new spine as beats (one line each) and get a quick nod.
4. **Rewrite** applying the lenses + craft layer.
5. **Final prose pass** with the humanizer (see below).
6. **Show your work briefly**: what you changed structurally and which symbol now carries the meaning. Keep it short.

## Workflow B — Generating from an idea

1. **Run Step 0.**
2. **Find the lens** (angle) and **the ending** (last line / the change) first.
3. **Lay out the spine** as beats connected by but/therefore. Confirm with the user.
4. **Choose the central symbol** that will carry the meaning and recur.
5. **Draft**, zooming into the key moment(s) rather than summarizing.
6. **Final prose pass** with the humanizer.

## Final prose pass — use the humanizer

Structure and symbolism make a piece *good*; the humanizer makes sure it doesn't *sound like AI wrote it*. After the story is built, run the draft through the humanizer to strip AI tells (significance-inflation, rule-of-three, em-dash overuse, superficial -ing phrases, generic upbeat endings) and to add genuine voice. For this user's honest, personal register, this pass is essential — a structurally perfect post still fails if it reads like a press release.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- **"And then" chains** — no causality, no stakes. Always test for but/therefore.
- **Announced transformations** — telling the reader you changed instead of earning it through the struggle.
- **Abstract-only writing** — no concrete image to hold the meaning. Every key idea needs a symbol or a moment.
- **Explaining the symbol** — if you built it right, the explanation is redundant. Cut it.
- **Decorative archetypes** — mythic structure pasted on without real specifics underneath reads as cliché.
- **Skipping Step 0** — never assume the format or voice. Ask.
- **Stopping before the humanizer pass** — a well-built story that still sounds like AI hasn't landed.
